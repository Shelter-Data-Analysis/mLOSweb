mLOS Web: length of stay for animal shelters

How long do animals stay in your shelter?

Load your shelter's records (a spreadsheet with a row for each animal's stay), answer a few questions about it, and get charts, tables, and a slide deck that answer questions such as these:

  • Are animals staying longer or shorter than they used to, for example since a new program or policy?
  • Which animals stay longest: strays, owner surrenders, or transfers in? Large dogs or small ones?
  • Once an animal has been with you for 20 days, how much longer is it likely to stay, and are its likely outcomes different from a new arrival's?
  • How do the animals you see on kennel rounds differ from the animals arriving at intake?

You do not need to install anything, and your records stay private: everything runs inside this browser tab, and nothing is sent anywhere. To try it first, use one of the demo files in step 1.

For analysts: the programs behind this page, and what it leaves out

The page prepares the records with ShelterDataPrep (Python) and analyzes them with mLOS (R), a length-of-stay analysis tool that uses survival analysis rather than simple averages. Both run unchanged inside this browser tab.

This page runs a subset of mLOS. It leaves out the Weibull regression (parametric_regression) and the confidence bounds of the conditional outcome curves (aj_conditional_ci); it compares at most four periods, six intake groups, and six animal groups drawn from one column; and it builds the slide deck with its default settings.

The full mLOS runs on your own computer, with R. Install it from GitHub or from its archive at Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22083814. The mLOS settings file this page writes runs there as is, and its ShelterDataPrep configuration runs there once its folders are pointed at your own.

Step 1

Load your shelter's records

A spreadsheet from your shelter's software, saved as an Excel workbook (.xlsx or .xlsm) or as a CSV file, with one row for each animal's stay and a row of column names above them. To try the page first, use a demo file: each is a real shelter's records, as published for research, with its source named beside the button.

Back with the files from an earlier run? (a ShelterDataPrep configuration or an mLOS settings file)

Each run saves two files that record its answers: a ShelterDataPrep configuration, for steps 2 to 6, and an mLOS settings file, for the choices in step 8. Load either, or both, with the same records to give the same answers again. A file this page wrote fills in the answers, to check or change; one edited by hand, or prepared for the programs on your own computer, runs as written, except for the changes the page lists.

Step 2

Say which column is which

Every shelter's software names its columns its own way. For each item below, pick the column of your file that holds it. The page fills in its best guesses; check them against the first rows of your file, shown above.

Step 3

Choose the animals to study

The analysis looks at one species at a time: pick the column that names the species, then the species to study. Then list any records that are not real shelter stays, such as animals dead on arrival, quarantine holds, or animals boarded for another agency. The page sets them aside before anything else.

Records that are not shelter stays

Ways to compare animals

Tick the columns you may want to compare animals by, such as size or sex. Step 8 compares by one of them, and can switch to another without starting over. Listed are your file's columns with 2 to 100 different values, and the age group, which step 6 works out from the date of birth.

Step 4

Choose the date format

The page tries each format on every date in the three date columns. The right format reads all of them. When two formats both read every date, the file cannot tell them apart: check a date you know.

Step 5

Set the study window

The span of time to study, from its first day up to, but not including, its stop date. Every stay that overlaps it is kept, including animals already in care on the first day. The analysis later divides this window into periods.

Step 6

Define the age groups

Age at intake is computed from the date of birth. Each group runs up to and including its upper age; an animal older than the last group is dropped, and one with no date of birth is counted as _UNKNOWN_.

Group nameUp to age (years)

Leave a name blank to drop that row.

Step 7

Prepare the data

These choices become a ShelterDataPrep configuration file. Its other rules are not asked about: they are taken from ShelterDataPrep's configuration for Mission Viejo and are the same for every file, and "Show the configuration file" lists them. One of them renames size labels written the Mission Viejo way, such as LARGE, and changes nothing in a file that does not use them. Outcome and intake labels pass through unchanged and are classified in step 8.

    Step 8
    
    Run the analysis
    
    
      
      
      Show the settings file as it will run
      
    
      
    
      Classify the outcomes
      
    
      Each outcome label in the prepared data, with the number of stays that end in it. Choose what each one means. Guesses are filled in where the label is a common one; every label needs a choice before the analysis can run.
      
    
      
    
      
    
      Group the intake types
      
    
      Each intake label in the prepared data, with its number of stays. Give each one a group name; labels with the same name are combined into one group, and the analysis compares the groups. Up to six groups. Tick "Leave out" to study only the other intakes.
      
    
      
    
    
      
    
      Set the periods
      
    
      mLOS compares periods within the study window. Add up to three dividing dates to split it into as many as four periods, and name each one. Choose the dates before looking at the results: at real events where there are any (a new program, a policy change), otherwise at calendar years or half-years. Aim for at least 100 outcomes in each period.
      
    
      
      Dividing dates (each starts a new period):
      
      
      
    
      
    
      
    
      
    
      Group the animals
      
    
      Choose one of the columns carried from step 3 to compare animals by. Each of its values, with its number of stays, gets a group name; values with the same name are combined into one group. Up to six groups. Tick "Leave out" to study only the other animals.
      
    
      
      
      
    
      
    
      
    
    
      
    
      Set the analysis horizon
      
    
      The longest stay the analysis follows (restricted_stay_cap). A stay that reaches it counts as in care up to that day, and nothing after it is used. Too low, and real long stays are cut short; too high, and a few mistaken records can dominate the averages. Start at 365 days, then check the fraction still in care at the horizon, which the results report after the run.
      
    
      
      
      
    
      
    
      
    
      Choose the reference groups
      
    
      Each hazard ratio compares a group with its reference. The largest group is usually the right one; choose another large group if the ratios come out extreme or their intervals very wide.
      
    
      
      
      
    
      
    
      
    
      !
      
      Stay on this tab while mLOS runs.Do not switch to another tab, window, or program until the results appear. Browsers slow down, and can pause, a tab that is out of sight.
      
    
    
      
    
      
  
      

  
      
    Step 9
    
      Build the slide deck and its tables
      
    
      This step runs a second program, the deck builder mlos_review, which comes with mLOS and reads the analysis results. It writes a PowerPoint deck of findings and a workbook of the tables the deck draws on. On your own computer the same builder runs as python3 -m mlos_review.deck.
      
    
    
    
      
    
      
  
      

  
      
    Step 10
    
      Save everything
      
    
      One zip file holding every file this page made: the prepared data, the mLOS results, and the slide deck and its tables if they were built. The ShelterDataPrep configuration and the mLOS settings file sit at the top of the zip, ready to reuse. Nothing is kept once this tab is closed or reloaded.
